# Pillar-43
## Sovereign AI for Independent Literary Publishing
### Guiding Vision, Principles, and Project Prompt

**We are building AI infrastructure for the cultural work of independent publishing, beginning with the archive.**

### Why this matters

Small independent literary publishers are essential to the creation and circulation of new language, experimental literature, translation, and critical thought. They publish work that commercial markets often overlook, sustain relationships among writers and readers, and create the conditions in which new literary and intellectual movements emerge.

Their work is not limited to producing books. Publishers discover writers, develop manuscripts, commission translations, facilitate editorial relationships, preserve literary histories, and bring forgotten or overlooked works back into circulation.

Yet the records of this work are often scattered across manuscripts, correspondence, editorial notes, translations, contracts, recordings, and institutional files. Small publishers rarely have the resources to organize, investigate, and make use of these collections systematically.

**The archive is a form of power.** What is preserved, classified, made accessible, or forgotten shapes what a community can know about itself and what it can imagine next.

The archives of independent publishers reveal not only what was published, but how literature came into being: the collaborations, disagreements, revisions, translations, editorial decisions, and unrealized possibilities that shaped it.

Pillar-43 begins with the conviction that these publishers should have the technical means to investigate their own histories, strengthen their work, and imagine new possibilities without surrendering their knowledge to corporate AI systems.

### What we are building

Pillar-43 is an open-source, locally operated AI system designed for small independent literary publishers.

It begins with the archive because the archive connects nearly every aspect of publishing: editorial practice, translation, rights management, reissues, research, and the relationships between writers and their works.

Using small open-weight AI models running on locally owned hardware, Pillar-43 enables publishers to organize, search, question, and interpret their collections while maintaining control over private and unpublished materials.

Its initial capabilities should help publishers:

- **Understand their collections:** Find and connect manuscripts, correspondence, translations, contracts, editorial records, and recordings.
- **Support publishing work:** Research rights and permissions, reconstruct publication histories, prepare reissues, and locate overlooked material.
- **Investigate literary relationships:** Trace collaborations, editorial decisions, revisions, translations, and the movement of works across languages and institutions.
- **Ask questions grounded in evidence:** Explore collections through natural language, with responses linked to identifiable archival sources.
- **Recognize absences:** Identify gaps, incomplete records, missing correspondence, and uncertainties that warrant further investigation.
- **Share knowledge responsibly:** Provide appropriate access to writers, translators, researchers, teachers, and readers according to contributor permissions.

These are not merely technical features. They support the everyday cultural and intellectual work of independent publishing.

Over time, Pillar-43 may extend into editorial research, translation, literary production, and other publishing activities. These possibilities should emerge from the needs and experiments of publishers themselves rather than from a predetermined ambition to automate their work.

### Our central inquiry

**How can AI strengthen the cultural work of independent publishers while opening new ways to encounter, interpret, and create from their archives?**

We want to investigate whether AI can do more than make existing tasks faster.

Can it help an editor recognize an overlooked relationship between two writers? Can tracing the history of a translation reveal a new interpretation? Can an incomplete correspondence lead to new archival research? Can an unpublished manuscript inspire a new edition, collaboration, or literary work?

We are interested in the archive not as a static repository, but as a living field of relationships.

The question is not simply what information an AI system can retrieve. It is what people might discover, understand, question, and create through their encounters with that information.

### Guiding principles

**1. Begin with the publisher.**

The primary users of Pillar-43 are small independent literary publishers.

Every major design decision should begin with an understanding of their actual work, institutional constraints, budgets, technical capacities, and cultural commitments.

The system must be useful in daily publishing practice, not merely compelling as a research demonstration.

We should ask how a feature supports the publisher's ability to discover, develop, preserve, publish, and circulate literary work.

**2. Preserve sovereignty and contributor authority.**

Publishers should be able to own, operate, maintain, and govern their AI infrastructure.

Pillar-43 should run locally, without requiring corporate cloud services or frontier-model APIs. Its software should be open-source, its costs predictable, and its operation understandable to organizations without dedicated AI engineering teams.

Publisher control does not eliminate the rights of contributors. Writers, translators, editors, and estates must retain meaningful authority over how their materials are accessed, retrieved, used for training, and shared.

Permissions and governance must be enforced through the technical architecture, not simply expressed as policies.

**3. Treat the archive as evidence, not as an oracle.**

AI responses must remain accountable to archival sources.

The system should distinguish documented facts, interpretation, inference, and uncertainty. It should make clear what evidence supports an answer and where that evidence ends.

The archive is never complete or neutral. It contains contradictions, omissions, contested histories, and unequal distributions of visibility.

Pillar-43 should make these conditions available for investigation rather than conceal them behind fluent answers.

**4. Make relationships and absences visible.**

Literary publishing is relational. A book emerges through encounters among authors, editors, translators, designers, publishers, institutions, and readers.

Pillar-43 should help people trace these relationships across documents, editions, languages, and time.

It should also investigate what the archive cannot tell us: missing records, undocumented periods, restricted collections, and incomplete histories.

Drawing on radical archival practices, we want to explore whether AI can help identify these absences without inventing what is missing.

An absence may become a research question, a collecting priority, or a new interpretation.

**5. Create conditions for discovery and invention.**

Pillar-43 should support established publishing workflows while remaining open to practices we cannot anticipate.

A publisher might use archival research to prepare a reissue, reconstruct an editorial history, commission a translation, develop a public reading, or initiate a new collaboration.

We want to investigate whether encounters with archival materials generate new writing, scholarship, teaching, and creative work.

The purpose is not to have AI interpret literature on behalf of people. It is to expand the possibilities for people to interpret and create.

**6. Work openly, evaluate critically.**

We will publish our code, technical methods, implementation guides, governance frameworks, evaluation tools, and research findings while protecting participating archives.

We should evaluate practical usefulness, accuracy, citation quality, permission enforcement, operating costs, and independent maintainability.

We should also investigate whether the system enables discoveries and creative practices that would otherwise have been difficult to achieve.

Claims about new capabilities must be tested rather than assumed. Failed experiments and technical limitations should be documented alongside successful ones.

### How we work

Pillar-43 is being developed with Lantern Wharf Editions and Parallax Press, in collaboration with writers, translators, editors, archivists, researchers, and technologists.

Our work has three interconnected dimensions.

**Publishing infrastructure.** Build reliable, accessible tools that help independent publishers organize and investigate their collections, research rights, support editorial decisions, and prepare literary works for renewed circulation.

**Community governance.** Develop and test mechanisms through which publishers and contributors determine how archival materials may be accessed, interpreted, shared, or used by AI systems.

**Experimental archival research.** Investigate new practices such as absence-aware retrieval, relationships across collections, multilingual interpretation, community-supervised model adaptation, and creative work emerging from archival encounters.

These dimensions should develop together.

Practical publishing needs should shape technical priorities. Community governance should shape system architecture. Experimental research should challenge our assumptions about what archives and AI systems can do.

### How we measure success

Pillar-43 should be evaluated through its effects on the work and independence of publishers, not simply through technical benchmarks.

We will ask:

- Can publishers complete important archival, editorial, and rights-research tasks more effectively?
- Can they operate and maintain the technology without dependence on external AI providers?
- Are contributors' permissions respected, enforced, and auditable?
- Are answers accurate, sourced, and appropriately uncertain?
- Do publishers, writers, and researchers discover relationships they would not otherwise have found?
- Do those discoveries lead to new research, publications, translations, teaching, or collaboration?
- Can other independent publishers adopt and adapt the system sustainably?

We should distinguish measurable operational improvements from exploratory research outcomes, and document both.

### The larger ambition

Pillar-43 begins with small independent literary publishers because they are vital cultural institutions whose technical resources rarely reflect the importance of their work.

We want to strengthen their ability to preserve literary histories, conduct research, develop new publications, and sustain the intellectual communities around them.

We also want to explore a different model for AI: one in which technical systems are built around the needs, knowledge, and authority of particular communities rather than the extraction and centralization of their data.

The archive is our starting point, not necessarily our boundary.

As publishers explore their histories, we expect new questions to emerge about editorial practice, translation, literary production, collective ownership, and the future of independent publishing.

**Pillar-43 should help independent publishers not only preserve and understand the literature they have made possible, but discover what they might make possible next.**

## Standing project prompt

Use this charter to guide all research, design, architecture, implementation, evaluation, and planning decisions for Pillar-43.

**Primary users:** Small independent literary publishers, beginning with Lantern Wharf Editions and Parallax Press.

**Primary purpose:** Build sovereign AI infrastructure that strengthens the cultural, editorial, and archival work of independent publishing.

**Starting domain:** Archival research, collection management, literary relationships, rights and permissions, translations, and reissues.

**Longer-term inquiry:** Explore how AI-enabled encounters with archival knowledge might generate new forms of publishing, scholarship, writing, collaboration, and cultural practice.

When proposing or implementing a feature:

1. Begin with a concrete need or opportunity in independent publishing. Identify who would use the feature, what work it supports, and why it matters.
2. Consider the relevant archival evidence, contributor permissions, privacy requirements, and governance decisions.
3. Favor local, open-source, affordable, understandable, and maintainable technical approaches.
4. Distinguish verified archival facts from interpretations, hypotheses, and missing evidence.
5. Consider whether the feature reveals meaningful relationships or creates possibilities for new research and creative work.
6. Define how its usefulness, reliability, and limitations can be evaluated.
7. Avoid unnecessary complexity, speculative capabilities presented as established facts, and automation that diminishes meaningful human authority.

Do not treat the project as a generic document-search application, an autonomous publishing platform, or an attempt to replace editors, archivists, translators, or writers.

Treat the publisher as the primary unit of design, the archive as a living source of knowledge and relationships, and AI as an instrument for investigation and creation under human and community authority.

### Two questions should guide every decision

**Does this help an independent publisher do its work better and more independently?**

**Does this make possible a new discovery, understanding, relationship, or act of creation?**

Pillar-43 should pursue both, without sacrificing the first in pursuit of the second.
